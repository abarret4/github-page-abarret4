What Is This Site?

During the Korean War (1950-1953), filmmakers in the United States produced a total of 32 movies related to the war effort abroad. Twenty-three ofthese films had the direct involvement of the newly-formed Department of Defense. This project highlights these 32 films: ongoing themes, messages, relations to the Korean War, and influence from the Department of Defense -- as well as the IMDB ratings for each film, for those looking for something to watch.

What sort of Korean War films did the Department of Defense actively produced while the war effort was ongoing? How can we see the propaganda in these films today? These are the questions this site seeks to answer.

Why It Matters

The Korean War is one of the least researched eras in the history of the relationship between Hollywood and the United States military, despite acting as a bridge between World War II and the broader Cold War. This site aims to explore this moment in history, elaborating on how the DOD intervened in Hollywood during the Korean War effort. This elaboration adds necessary context not just for how the relationship between Hollywood and the American military developed over time, but for how it functions today.
