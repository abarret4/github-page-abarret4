# Reflection

Even after all the trouble I've had with technology over the course of this semester, building a repository of static html files still managed to feel more complicated than I had expected. I had gone into this project with the idea that I wanted to create something that I'd thought was rather simple, but it turned out that most of my original plans were so complicated that I'd had no idea where to even start with them, and I ended up with a final product that consisted of way less than I had originally intended. I am still happy with my project, however, because I feel like I've learned a lot about just how complicated even the most simple-looking tasks in forming a website can really be.

One of the main things that I really struggled with in making this website was displaying images correctly on the website. Actually uploading the pictures I wanted onto the site was relatively simple, but figuring out how to lay them out correctly on the screen the way I wanted to was surprisingly difficult: I had a lot of trouble figuring out how to work with containers, in particular. Things got even more complicated when I wanted to portray different groups of images in different ways: it was surprisingly hard for me to figure out how to make the style sheet apply to some images and not others, and I had a lot of difficulty getting the "class" element to work the way I wanted it to. Then, after all this, I had to make sure that everything looked nice on laptop and mobile alike. I did not have too much trouble with this step, fortunately, but it did really drive home how many small, easy-to-look-over details about how websites work often take a lot of work to function correctly -- and how much it stands out when they fail to blend properly into the layout of the page. It was also interesting to recognize some of the errors that I was coming across from websites I had visited in the past, especially sites that I know had been created by amateur coders. It left me feeling a sense of kinship with the people who created those sites, and an understanding of how difficult they had probably been working to just make their site even remotely functional, when before I had admittedly assumed that they had simply been lazy and had not bothered trying to look over their work. It also gave me ideas about what aspects of their site's graphic designs they had struggled with and even how they may have been able to fix them. I can imagine that I will remember this project whenever I come across a site that I believe does not function correctly in the future. I hope that I will be able to understand how a person could actually improve these sites, given the opportunity.

Another thing I struggled with was the manipulation of my json file. In my efforts to build my site, I created a json file for my films that had all the data I wished to include alongside each film. When I tried to create a table that presented information from that file direcly, however, I found that I could not figure out how to access information from a json file in HTML. I tried consulting with AI, but it didn't help, and I could not figure it out through the w3schools website either.

One thing that really helped me figure out how to organize my website was actively drawing out what I wanted it to look like with a pen and paper, as opposed to simply trying to work from my imagination. I was surprised by how much it helped me figure out how to organize my actual html code once I had a physical layout in front of me to work from, even when that layout was a rather shaky sketch of a very basic layout. I had not expected it to help me organize my thoughts and put them to code as much as it did. I will probably remember this experience going forward even when it comes to non code-related things. Organizing a physical space, outlining the structure of a paper, preparing for an event -- though I may not have considered it before, after this experience I wouldn't be surprised if taking a moment to draw out a physical sketch would help me with tasks even outside of graphic design. Even if I believe that I know how I want a final product to look, physically sketching a plan out ahead of time to work from will probably help me a lot more than I would normally assume.

I really wanted to avoid using AI for my project as much as I could. While I did consult AI for a number of my exercises throughout the semester in an effort to complete specific tasks, I wanted this final project to be something that I did myself, even if that would mean I'd have to compromise on the complexity of the final product. Even then, I did attempt to use AI to figure out tasks that I was struggling with a couple of times (especially related to reading my json file in html), but the results I got confused me more than they helped me figure out what I wanted to do, so I ended up not using AI even for this.

If I were to continue this project, I would flesh out the rest of the html files related to each of the 32 films: currently, only *The Steel Helmet*, *Korea Patrol*, *Tokyo File 212*, and *A Yank in Korea* have their own html pages, and only *The Steel Helmet* has a page that is remotely complete -- and even then, I had so much trouble with the formatting that I was not able to include everything I wanted to. In particular, I would have liked to include clips from the actual film alongside transcripts of what was being said in the clips. I also would have liked to pull information from the .json file directly, so that if someone were to edit the .json file they would see the data edited right on the page.

The main thing I think I learned from doing this project had to do with graphic design and the style sheet. So much of my time was spent trying to organize this repository in a way that made it look professional: just the visual organization took way more time and effort than I had expected. Even things that I had assumed would be relatively simple often ended up taking me hours to figure out. While I'm proud that I managed to figure out what I did -- I know firsthand how hard it was for me, and I'm really happy that I managed to do even this much without help from AI -- it really is disheartening that I could only manage to do a fraction of what I had initially planned. I want to keep working at this so that I can get to a place where I am genuinely somewhat proficient in this field: I think it would be useful not just for employment, but for a number of my personal projects. I really want to get to the point where I can at least have fun experimenting with code.

I know that this final product is very simple, but it took a lot for me to get even this much, and I'm happy that it came out as a legible final product.
