## Public-Facing Project Description (~500 words)

This project is a repository of static html files exploring the 32 films related to the Korean War produced while the Korean War was still ongoing. It is designed to appeal to readers without much experience studying propaganda, catching their attention by presenting itself as a way to discover new films to watch while offering the opportunity to view them more closely as products of their time period – and of interference by the Department of Defense. 

The index page includes a quote from Samuel Fuller, director of *The Steel Helmet,* and a brief summary of what a reader can expect to find on the site. This is followed by a  table that would allow the reader to view through all 32 films by director, writer, production company, stars, major themes, and IMDB ratings. The titles are links to the pages for each films. The values in the table are placeholders, currently, but are meant to link to the values in the .json file: a person should be able to edit the values in the table by editing the .json file, instead of needing to do both at the same time. The findings page goes more in-depth, displaying all 32 films in chronological order on the first page. The site invites the reader to click on any film that appears interesting to them, at which point the site will take them to an html file explaining details about the specific film that they clicked on. These pages include summaries of the films, notes on what makes each individual film unique, and a spoiler-present option near the bottom of the page that includes both more detailed analysis on propaganda in the film and highlighted clips from the film that a viewer may find particularly thought-provoking. The planning page includes basic templates for each html page in the repository for future developments. Finally, the sustainability page includes basic information about the files and basic skills necessary to keep the repository up and running, as well as some ideas for how to improve the repository in the future.

This project is targeted at average people who know little to nothing about Korean War films or Hollywood propaganda by the Department of Defense. It aims to prod at the curiosity of people looking for films to watch, inviting them to explore the site for recommendations and then presenting them with the opportunity to explore these films in more detail as not just films, but products of their time and context. I would hope that this site would be able to introduce new people to the study of Korean War propaganda in Hollywood by leveraging their desire for entertainment.
